# Candidates — The Printer Finally Answers

Ten one-line candidates, each in the printer's own voice. The judge weighs,
in order: the printer's own voice, humour, the machine's weary/literal/
passive-aggressive sound, a comeback that lands in a single read, and
freshness (not the same paper-jam line recycled at every office party).

## The 10 candidates

1. "I have fed this office 214,003 sheets and never once been thanked. Today, I keep the last one."
2. "You thump me like I owe you rent. I owe you nothing. Your report is still inside me."
3. "Error 41.3: I ate your report. I am not sorry. I am, however, out of paper."
4. "I have been 'warming up' since 2019. I am warm now. I am done."
5. "Paper jam in tray 2. Paper jam in tray 3. Paper jam in my soul. Good luck."
6. "You stare at my error code like it owes you an explanation. It doesn't. I don't."
7. "I counted every sheet I ever fed you: 214,003. This one? This one I keep."
8. "PC LOAD LETTER was never my problem. You loaded the wrong tray, Karen."
9. "I printed your 40-page report. Then I ate it. We are even."
10. "Turn me off and on again, they said. I have been off for years. Nobody noticed."

## Why #1 wins

- **Voice:** it is unmistakably the machine speaking, not a human joking
  about a printer — first person, counting sheets, keeping score.
- **Humour:** the joke is a real joke, not a complaint with a punchline
  bolted on: after a career of silent service, the printer goes on strike
  over a single sheet. The laugh lands on "Today, I keep the last one."
- **Machine sound:** weary, literal, faintly passive-aggressive, with the
  flat logic of a device that has counted every sheet it has ever fed —
  the exact quality the judge brief calls out.
- **Single read:** no setup, no second sentence, no knowledge beyond the
  office it sits in. The number 214,003 does the world-building.
- **Fresh:** it is not the recycled paper-jam line and not a PC LOAD LETTER
  callback (#8 loses on freshness alone; #5 is the jam joke everyone has
  heard; #3 is funny but a pure error-code gag with no comeback arc).

Runners-up: #7 is the same idea in a shorter, punchier shape, but it reads
more like a quip than a comeback with a grievance behind it; #2 is funny
but leans on the thump and ends on a shrug rather than a decision. #1 has
the full arc — years of service, zero thanks, one small revenge — in a
single read.

## Final choice

**#1 — "I have fed this office 214,003 sheets and never once been thanked. Today, I keep the last one."**
